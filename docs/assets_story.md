# The assets story — what you bring, and what the tree makes

You arrive with a game idea. This page is what that idea needs in the way of
art and sound, what this repo generates for you, and the rules for anything
imported from outside. The short version: **almost everything on screen here
is program output** — you will mostly write small deterministic Python, not
draw — and the few imported pixels in the tree each carry a recorded grant, a
digest, and a README saying exactly what was done to them.

## 1. How a game gets its look here

**Generator-first.** A game's tiles, palettes, sprites, worlds, gradient
tables and LUTs are emitted by a `tools/gen_*.py` script — 53 of them at last
count (`gen_register.py`, the 54th, generates the feature register, not art)
— into `build/assets/`, at build time, every build. Nothing under `build/` is
committed. Three reasons, and they compound:

- **Determinism.** Every generator's header states the same contract:
  *byte-identical on re-run, pure integer math*. No floats at decision points
  (`tools/gen_iris_lut.py` uses exact `isqrt` specifically so the oracle
  cannot depend on the host's libm), no clocks, randomness only through a
  fixed seed, no dict-iteration-order dependence
  (`tools/gen_shmup_assets.py` spells the full list out). This is what lets a
  test import the generator as its oracle and assert rendered pixels against
  it, and what makes pinning a ROM's md5 worth anything.
- **Provenance.** Generated bytes have no licence question. The source of a
  procedural sky is the script, and the script is first-party.
- **Regeneration.** A look lives in one reviewable place. Change the
  constant, `make <game>`, and the blob, the ROM and the oracle-following
  tests all move together.

**The pipeline, end to end.** A generator emits a blob → the game or feature
declares a `rom` claim for it (`game.toml` / `feature.toml`) → the allocator
places the claim and emits symbols → the game's ASM `.incbin`s the blob under
`.assert`s that its bank and address equal the allocator's symbols (the blob
block at the top of `game/microzero/main.asm` is the shape; drift refuses the
link) → `make rom-unbacked` proves every `rom` claim is actually filled by an
`.incbin` ([`docs/37`](37_rom_claim_backing_gate.md)). An asset cannot be
placed by hand, and a claim cannot silently go hollow.

**Fonts.** One face is vendored: `vendor/fonts/unscii-8.hex` — unscii-8 by
viznut, public domain / CC0, a 96-glyph subset verified glyph-by-glyph
against upstream (`NOTICE`). Two generators consume it: `tools/gen_font.py`
(the 2bpp monospace tiles every text-drawing rail links) and
`tools/gen_vwf_font.py` (the VWF compositor's glyph masks + advance table —
the renderer holds no glyph data, so pointing these two scripts at a
different `.hex` and rebuilding is the entire face swap). What was examined
and rejected, and the still-open search for a licence-verified proportional
face, is [`docs/11`](11_font_assets.md).

**Audio.** `assets/audio/` is the audio content: a song authored as MML
(`mml/slice_b_song.mml`), sound effects (`sound-effects.txt`), and four
instrument samples all synthesised by `tools/gen_audio_samples.py` —
fixed-seed, byte-identical on re-run — because the standing rule there is
**no reference-sourced sample material, generate instead**. It is also the
one place generated output is deliberately checked in: the
`tad-compiler ca65-export` artifacts under `export/`, so the build never
needs a Rust toolchain. Regeneration is a documented one-command step whose
asserts refuse a partial update — `assets/audio/README.md` is the whole
story.

## 3. Re-skinning, and adding art of your own

**Tunables vs look contracts.** Most visual constants are freely tunable:
edit the generator, rebuild, and the tests that re-derive their expectations
from the generator follow your change. But some looks are **contracts**,
pinned on purpose by a fixture or an md5 — there, a changed look is a
decision, and a test goes red until the pin is deliberately updated with it:

- The platformer's dusk ramp:
  `test_the_dusk_ramp_matches_the_reference_ramp` in
  `tests/test_platformer.py` pins the declared endpoints and compares the
  blob to `tests/fixtures/ref_dusk_grad.bin`, a reference captured off a
  *second implementation's* running ROM — agreeing with it is not agreeing
  with ourselves, and retiring it is how a real re-skin ships. Its sibling
  `test_the_dusk_sky_on_screen_is_the_declared_ramp` stays green across a
  re-skin, because it asserts the *shape* of a dusk (red falls, blue rises),
  not the bytes. The pair splits exactly along the contract/tunable line.
- microzero's whole ROM is pinned by md5 (`tests/test_c2_sram_class.py`) —
  the strongest form: every byte of its look is a contract, because other
  measurements cite that exact binary.

A red pinning test after a colour change is the system working: it is the
interlock that finds every place the old look was still being asserted.

**The checklist for a visual change:**

1. **Find every home of the value before editing — grep by VALUE, not
   name** (ASM spells it `$1C8D`, Python `0x1C8D` — search the bare digits).
   A look constant is written where it is derived and restated where it is
   consumed and verified, under different names. The live example: the
   platformer's menu-backdrop word is derived as the dusk ramp's midpoint in
   `tools/gen_platformer_assets.py`, restated as `PLF_DUSK = $1C8D` in
   `game/platformer/platformer.inc`, and again as `0x1C8D` in the test
   driver `tests/plf_drive.py`. Grepping the name finds two homes; grepping
   the value finds all three. Edit the writers together, or a red test will
   name the missed one for you — correct, but slower.
2. **Read the generator's docstring before touching its constants.** The
   constraints live at the point of edit — some as asserts
   (`tools/gen_m7f_gradient.py` refuses a floor palette that drifts from its
   day snapshot, and refuses a haze the add-only COLDATA hardware cannot
   reach), some as prose (its neighbour-step tuning rule). The asserts will
   catch you; the prose you have to read.
3. **Rebuild, and check what moved.** The ROM's checksum/md5 must move — the
   change reached the artifact, the falsification harness's own standard —
   and the rail's `symbol_map.json` must **not**: a content change inside an
   existing claim alters no layout, and the allocator saying nothing is the
   demonstrable answer. If the map moved, you changed a claim instead.
4. **Run the look-facing tests and read the reds.** Expected outcome:
   oracle-following tests green, pins red. A red pin is never fixed by more
   syncing — it is a decision to record (update the fixture/pin with the new
   look) or to reverse.
5. **Done = a fresh render from the verified binary** (CLAUDE.md rule 3), not
   a green suite alone.

**Adding art means adding a generator**, and the bar it must meet is the one
the existing 53 document in their own headers:

- **Deterministic**: integer math; any randomness through a fixed-seed
  `random.Random` (the audio samples' precedent); nothing consults a clock,
  a float rounding, or an iteration order.
- **Stated outputs**: every emitted file, its size and its format named in
  the docstring — headers here read as contracts, and tests import the
  generator as their oracle for the rendered result.
- **Refuse, remap or assert — never mask**: an encoder may not `& 0x0F` an
  out-of-range pixel into range; a rejection names the offending coordinates
  (`tools/gen_platformer_assets.py`'s "NO SILENT MASKING" block states the
  rule both for imported pixels and authored grids).
- **Backed and wired**: emit into `build/assets/`, declare the `rom` claim,
  `.incbin` under the bank/address asserts, keep `make rom-unbacked` green,
  and give the Makefile recipe the generator as a prerequisite.

## 4. Importing, and the rules

**When import is allowed.** The rule carries a condition, and the condition
is load-bearing (AGENTS.md records the day a condition-free version of it
talked a session out of the *right* asset): not "don't import," but **don't
import an asset whose source format you would have to infer**. A documented
format from a named upstream, under a grant you can state from a primary
source, is importable. The usual reason to import at all is identity: the
platformer's hero and the brawler's knights are the artist's pixels because
hand-authored stand-ins were tried first and "they are not the same
characters" (`tools/gen_platformer_assets.py`, in its own header).

**The provenance chain, per pack** — four links, all in the tree:

1. **The original bytes, vendored unmodified** under `vendor/art/<pack>/` —
   never a re-save or re-export; renames only where recorded.
2. **A digest per file**: every vendored pack file is sha256-matched against
   its member of the upstream archive, recorded in
   [`docs/92`](92_provenance_audit.md) §5.1.
3. **The grant, read at its primary source** and recorded in `NOTICE` — a
   licence file sitting beside an asset is a claim, not evidence (NOTICE's
   own header), so no row rests on one alone. The recorded grants are exact,
   never flattened: camelot, dungeonSprites and the Four Seasons tileset
   are CC0; the Spaceship Pack is a page-stated "free for commercial
   use"; dizworld is CC BY 4.0 with attribution required — and reaches only
   the CPU-probe ROMs, never a game.
4. **A per-pack README** stating what each file is and what the generator
   does with it, plus **reference conversions kept as refusal oracles** —
   committed conversion outputs that the generator or its tests are held to,
   so a conversion cannot drift silently (`vendor/art/camelot/README.md` and
   `vendor/art/dungeon_sprites/README.md` are the pattern). One naming note:
   those oracles and the generator comments cite `png2snes`, the separate
   converter that produced them — it is deliberately **not part of this
   tree** (the cross-check is real only because the two derivations share no
   code), and its name with line numbers is kept as the record of which of
   its branches each generator reproduces.

Not everything under `vendor/art/` is third-party: several directories
(`m7_dungeon`, `mode7_chamber`, the `split_h_*` oracles, the
`platformer_stream` refs) are first-party artifacts frozen as oracles —
"Nothing here is a build input" (`vendor/art/m7_dungeon/README.md`). The
per-pack README says which kind you are looking at.

**What `make cleanroom` checks — a floor, not a ceiling.**
`tools/cleanroom_check.sh` states its own scope in its header, and this page
repeats it rather than improving on it: a fast, **non-exhaustive** denylist
scan of committed text — and of the text members of committed zips — for
retail-game, company and hardware-brand names, plus a forbidden-file class
(ROMs, music rips, emulator cores, commercially-named media) and a >2 MB
tripwire. A hit means "someone wrote a name that wants a second look," not
"a copyright was infringed" — and a pass is **not** a completeness
guarantee. The high-risk artifacts — copied assets and broken attribution —
are not something a grep can settle; they are guarded by `NOTICE`, the
per-pack READMEs, and reading the diff. The gate runs first in `make gates`,
prints its reach so a disarmed pass reads as disarmed, and its allowlist
(`tools/cleanroom_allow.txt`) takes conscious per-line exemptions only.

**The flat rules:**

- **No ripped assets.** Nothing extracted from a shipped game or record: no
  ROMs, no music or sample rips, no sprite rips. The cleanroom gate's
  forbidden-file classes enumerate the shapes; the audio pipeline's stronger
  form is that every sample is synthesised (`assets/audio/README.md`).
- **No commercial names.** Not in identifiers, filenames, comments or docs.
  Genre inspiration is welcome; the name is not.
- **Nothing whose licence you cannot state.** If you cannot write the asset's
  `NOTICE` row — upstream, grant, quote, digest — it does not come in. "It
  looked free" is not a statement, and neither is a licence file that
  shipped beside the asset.
